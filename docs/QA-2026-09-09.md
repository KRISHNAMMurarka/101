# Local QA — 9 September 2026

Environment: Apple M3 Max, 128 GiB RAM, Node 24.14.0, Codex in-app browser. Browser work used the local development server on localhost:3101. No external preview, deployment, push, or remote test service was used. The production build and server-render tests run separately.

## Route and layout evidence

Inspected 31 routes at 320×740, 740×320 and 2560×1440 in both themes: **186 DOM/keyboard-entry smoke checks**. Routes were the launcher, devices, controller, Studio and its seven tools, and the chooser/play pair for Slashstorm, TiltDrift, BodyDodge, Orbital Crew, BeatForge, GravityStack, Spellcaster, Echo Maze, Shadow Arena and Swarm Commander. Catalog bench used 50 entries.

Checks recorded document width, horizontal overflow, heading, computed theme background, and focus after Tab. The lazy InputLab initially had no heading during loading; a subsequent accessibility-tree and screenshot check confirmed its complete interface and focusable canvas. GravityStack similarly creates its canvas after initialization. These checks do not prove every gameplay action or a complete keyboard journey.

The matrix found VisionLab at 363px on a 320px viewport. Its diagram columns had intrinsic minimum widths; after the batch, columns were corrected and a 320px recheck showed scrollWidth=320. All other matrix cases had no document overflow. Earlier exploratory checks found launcher scrollWidth=353 at 320px; corrected grid minimums and retested at 320px before the matrix.

Representative screenshots were inspected for the launcher, result overlay, controller stress layouts, VisionLab, InputLab, and controller dock. This is a bounded layout review, not a claim of a pixel-by-pixel visual audit of every state.

## Interaction evidence

- A completed Slashstorm run originally left focus on CANVAS. The shared result panel now focuses the Play again button and associates the score with it. BodyDodge also showed the focused restart control.
- Light mode originally rendered the score in rgb(10,10,10) against a dark scrim. After correction, result text is rgb(242,242,242) and secondary text rgb(188,188,188). Dark canvas overlays now own their text palette independently from the page theme. Contract tests verify text/control contrast for both page themes and result scrims.
- An eight-button controller layout clipped/wrapped long labels outside their buttons at 320px. Container-relative labels and bounded wrapping corrected it; measured label overflow became zero. Center-only and two-left-pad layouts are covered by planner tests; two-pad browser layout remained within 320px.
- BodyDodge's dock opens below the canvas and feeds the existing host. At 740×320 fullscreen, measured canvas ended at y=122 and dock began at y=123: reserved space, no canvas overlap. The dock uses 197px here, leaving a small game field; physical landscape playability needs review. A subsequent completed run confirmed controls collapsed (aria-expanded=false), canvas height recovered to 265px, and focus moved to Play again. Portrait dock buttons measured 44–64px high; d-pad cells about 52.7px. Fullscreen target increased to 44px.
- An invalid pairing code previously showed raw ticket vocabulary and a Connected disclosure while the session said WAITING. It now says Connect to a game and gives a short scan/paste recovery instruction.
- Controller UX was rechecked against production builds at `localhost:3105` and `localhost:3106`. At 844×390, a phone without a role showed its room, connection action, and a plain next step rather than inactive game buttons. With Controller Lab's dense seven-control layout at 740×320, `scrollHeight` equalled `innerHeight` and every control remained visible: shoulder/movement on the left, two analog actions plus face buttons on the right, and the centred toggle between them. Switching to left-handed mode mirrored only the thumb clusters and kept the toggle centred. At 390×844, repeated control labels were removed and face buttons filled their portrait thumb zone; at 320×740, neither axis overflowed. Activating the toggle set the host's normalized `lock` action to `true`. This is local browser/emulation evidence; physical touch, grip, and haptic acceptance remain pending.

## Camera and performance evidence

The chooser enumerated a built-in camera and a phone continuity camera. The built-in camera started successfully. Use this camera advanced to placement; actual inference produced “We can only see part of you” and corrective instructions. Tab/Return advanced to Check, and keyboard Skip returned to the chooser. Heading focus now follows each setup beat and returns to the chooser on cancellation. Navigation stops the owned adapter; no recording is enabled.

VisionLab showed active real pose inference at **10.9 ms** in one unthrottled snapshot and **16.4 ms** in one snapshot with CDP CPU throttling set to 4×. These are individual observations on the M3 Max, using the bundled lite model, not mean/p95 measurements, end-to-end frame latency, a phone benchmark, or proof of complete placement/gesture confirmation. GPU work is not proportionally slowed by CPU emulation. CPU throttling was reset to 1× afterward.

Physical final gestures, the eight-second alternate step, person crossings, unplug/permission-denial recovery, selected-camera persistence through a real completed walkthrough, and complete play with/without camera have not been certified. Fake-stream/backend integration tests exercise the actual adapter/bus/game path but cannot establish physical accuracy. See VISION-VALIDATION.md for fixture/producer review.

## Production and automated checks

The prior built stylesheet referenced /_next/static/fonts/*.woff2 while files existed only under /fonts. The emitted-font test was observed failing with ENOENT before the fix. Fonts now enter Vite as source-managed assets. The offline stamper also had to recognize Controller as a shared chunk after the game dock began importing it.

The offline controller package includes a hashed HTML shell and 31 dependent assets, including four fonts. Atomic install failures preserve the prior version; no forced takeover interrupts its clients. Tests execute worker handlers against a mock CacheStorage and fetch implementation, including a version transition.

The production controller was also checked in a local browser at `localhost:3102`: `/sw.js` registered with `/controller` scope, took control after one reload, and then loaded `/controller?session=OFFLINE99` with browser networking disabled. It displayed `OFFLINE99`, rather than the cached shell's `101LAB` room, and its controller controls remained available. Browser networking was restored immediately after the check. This proves a current-build offline reopen and URL hydration path. It does not prove an installed-device upgrade between two different production builds.

A cross-build controller lifecycle was then exercised in a fresh local browser origin at `localhost:3103`. Build A from `5d9e35a` installed cache `101-link-15a8dc66c0b6c53f`; build B from the source of `b1953d5` installed `101-link-6664efd0a6b493b9` while the A client remained active. The newer worker waited rather than taking control. With browser networking disabled, the active A client reloaded to `BUILD-A` and retained usable controls. After every A-controlled page closed, B activated, removed the obsolete A cache, and reopened offline at `BUILD-B-OFFLINE` with the requested room and controls. Networking was restored and the temporary browser closed. This is real desktop-browser service-worker evidence; it does not replace an installed-PWA check on a physical device.

The offline hydration review also found that a cached shell could retain session101LAB from its server props. The controller now waits for the current URL snapshot before creating a transport, preserving valid case/hyphens and clearing absent cached pairing data. Five regressions cover that route boundary.

## Keyboard journeys — 25 September 2026

Method: a production build served by `npm start`. A scripted, headless Chrome 154 session with a
fresh profile sent real key events through the DevTools protocol, never clicks. Every step moved
focus with Tab or Shift+Tab and activated with Enter or Space. After every stop the script recorded
the focused element, whether it matched `:focus-visible`, whether it drew an outline or ring, and
whether it was on screen. Focus sweeps ran with reduced motion so that smooth scrolling had finished
before the position was read. The camera steps used Chrome's synthetic camera, which covers
reachability and focus only. It cannot stand in for a person, so the Confirm step and the
eight-second alternative are still pending (see below).

**Every game, start to restart.** For each of the ten games, the script went library card → chooser
→ Start → play field focused → run ends → result → Play again → play field focused again → Back to
the chooser. Eight games end on their own if left alone. GravityStack was played by dropping pieces
and tilting gravity. Echo Maze was played by walking the clue's shortest-path distances into echoes,
because an idle maze run never ends by design; a run took 6–7 minutes.

All of the following ran on the final build of this change set:

| Size and theme | Games start to restart | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1280×800, dark | 10 of 10 | Space restarts every game, and the play field regains focus after every restart. |
| 1280×800, light | 10 of 10 | Same. |
| 390×844, dark, touch | 10 of 10 | Cards below the first screen come in as focus reaches them. |
| 844×390, light, touch | 10 of 10 to restart | Back is hidden by design in short landscape, see below. |

Focus sweeps Tabbed through every stop on all 32 routes at 1280×800 (dark) and 390×844 (light).
Every stop was visible, outlined and on screen. No page error or console error was logged, apart
from the refused Hub connection noted below.

**What the journeys found and what changed:**

| Found | Fixed |
| --- | --- |
| Space did not press a focused button in any game. The keyboard adapter cancelled Space and the arrow keys on the whole window, so **Play again** answered only Enter, and the press was also read as a jump. | The adapter leaves a key to any focused control that handles keys itself: buttons, links, form fields, editable text and ARIA widgets. A unit test was observed failing first. Space then restarted all ten games. |
| GravityStack never gave its play field focus. The stage was an unfocusable image, so focus stayed on the page for the whole run. | The stage is focusable, focused on start like the other nine canvases, and outlined when focused. A rendered test now requires every play route to have a focusable field whose label names its keys. |
| **Turn on the camera** disabled itself while the camera started, which dropped focus to the page. "Use this camera" and **Try again** after a refused permission had the same problem. | The button stays enabled, marked `aria-disabled` and ignoring presses while starting. Focus now stays on it until **Use this camera**. |
| **Reset** in the library disabled itself, and the empty-state reset unmounted, so focus fell to the page. | Reset returns focus to the search field. |
| The pairing dialog was `aria-modal` in name only. Focus stayed on the button that opened it, Tab walked the page behind it, and Escape did nothing. | It is a native modal `<dialog>`. Focus starts on Close, the page behind is inert, Escape closes it, and focus returns to the opener. |
| Escape in the on-screen controller dock closed it but dropped focus to the page, because the focused control unmounted. | Focus returns to **Show controls**. |

Checked and fine: library search, the input filter and its announced count; **Connect a phone**
toggles and reports `aria-expanded`. Camera setup's Camera, Where to stand and Check steps each put
focus on their heading. Skip and Play another way return focus to "How do you want to play?". A
refused camera shows its cause, and **Try again** and **Play another way** are reachable. The dock's
controls scroll into view with a visible ring, and the play field regains focus after every restart.

Not a product fault: with the local Hub not running, the pairing dialog shows "Hub offline" and the
browser logs the refused connection.

Two known limits:

- The library grid renders the rows near the screen. On a one- or two-column phone layout, each
  card that takes focus brings the next rows in on the following frame. Separate Tab presses reach
  every game, but holding Tab down (auto-repeat) can outrun the render and skip to the footer.
- Short landscape hides each game's heading, including Back, to give the game the screen. The tab
  bar is hidden there too, so the way out is the browser's own back.

Fixed while running these: the catalog spacer's row height (below), and a scroll padding that stops
a Tab press from leaving the focused control under the phone's fixed tab bar.

One new framework finding: MediaPipe's 11.7 MB `vision_wasm_internal.wasm` is served as
`application/octet-stream`. Chrome logs "wasm streaming compile failed" and falls back to compiling
from a downloaded buffer, so the camera still works but starts a little slower. The server strips a
configured `Content-Type` for static files and has no `.wasm` entry. vinext fixed this upstream in
#2713, first released in 1.0.0-beta.5; this repository uses beta.2. Upgrading the framework for this
alone was not done.

## Responsive layout audit — 25 September 2026

Method: the same production build and headless Chrome 154, dark theme and reduced motion. Every
route (the library, devices, controller, Studio and its seven tools, each game's chooser and play
page, and the not-found page) was loaded at 14 sizes. Those were 240×600, 282×860 with a mouse (the
width of the in-app browser pane), 320×568, 360×640, 390×844, 430×932, 568×320, 667×375, 844×390,
768×1024, 1024×768, 1280×800, 1440×900 and 1920×1080. Every size below 900px used a touch pointer
and a mobile user agent. That makes 448 pages. For each page a script reported:

- horizontal page scroll
- any text or control past the screen edge or outside its nearest visible container
- text clipped without an ellipsis
- text printed over other text, after cutting each text box by every ancestor that clips it
- content covered by the fixed tab bar at the end of the page
- touch targets under 24px

Separately, every canvas and page height was sampled twice, 1.5s apart, at 390, 768 and 1280px.
Screenshots of each flagged page were reviewed before and after the fix.

First pass: 273 distinct containment findings. The overlap check added later found 49 more. Final
pass: none, apart from one reviewed false positive, the Vision Lab's idle "33", whose tight line box
overlaps its caption's without the glyphs touching. No canvas or page grows.

| Severity | Found | Fixed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blocker | Below 1050px, **Echo Maze, GravityStack and Orbital Crew showed no play field.** Their grids folded to one column but kept `"stage rail"` areas, so the rail took an implicit second column at full width and the stage had zero width. | Areas fold with the columns. The controller dock's frame, which now wraps each stage, takes the stage area. A stylesheet contract fails for any one-column fold that keeps multi-column areas. |
| High | Above 760px, the same three games overflowed the one-screen layout. At 1280×800 GravityStack's instructions printed across its rail and the controls dock fell off the screen, and Orbital Crew's crew list ran over its key legend. | Beside the stage, the grid row is the room left, the stage shrinks into it and the rail scrolls. Stacked under the stage (760–1050px), the page scrolls. |
| High | Every game's score bar was forced into one row by a later `!important` rule that beat all ten per-game phone rules. At 320px the score ran into its neighbour and the last label was cut off. | Below 430px the stats wrap two to a row with even hairlines. Orbital Crew's score stacks, and on wider screens its label sits over its number. |
| High | The Studio Input Lab's canvas grew by about 5,000px a second at 768 and 1280px. A game rule's `height: 100%` resolved against a grid cell containing the canvas itself. | The lab keeps its fixed stage height. |
| Medium | Library card tags clipped at the card edge at 320, 768 and 1280px, hiding the "+N" count. | Labels shorten with an ellipsis. Icons and the count always show, and the full label stays in the markup and a title. |

Below 340px of card width the head stacks icon, name and state. The room code wraps under its label. |

Content below moved down, and a focused footer link ended up below the screen. | Spacers use the card's row height. The rendered benchmark test checks every column count against the stylesheet's row height. |

| Low | The Vision Lab's idle digits overlapped each other. | Looser tracking. |

Not covered: physical phones and tablets (the touch sizes above are emulated), Safari and Firefox,
and browser text-only zoom. The layout relies on container queries, `:has()` and `<dialog>`, which
current Safari and Firefox support. That support has not been exercised here.
